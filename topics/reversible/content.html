Reversible computing

The energy floor, and how to sneak under it

Every time an ordinary logic gate switches, the charge on its wires is dumped as heat. Physics also sets a deeper floor: in 1961 Rolf Landauer showed that erasing one bit of information must release at least kT ln 2 of heat, about 3 zeptojoules at room temperature. An AND gate erases information (from a 0 output you cannot tell which inputs made it). Reversible logic keeps every bit, so in principle it can avoid both losses: charge slowly, and recover the energy instead of throwing it away.

A wire of 1 fF driven through 1 kΩ. Ordinary switching loses ½CV² each time; ramping the voltage over a time much longer than RC loses about (RC/T)·CV², so slower means cooler. Real adiabatic chips need resonant power supplies to recover the energy, and leakage sets a limit on how slow they can go.

Toffoli gate: a, b, c → a, b, c XOR (a AND b)

Run the Toffoli gate backwards: pick an output

With c = 0, the Toffoli gate's third output is a AND b, and the inputs are still there: nothing was erased, so the gate can be run backwards. Reversible circuits pay for this with extra "garbage" outputs that must be uncomputed later.
