The community chip

Can learners make one real chip together?

A single Tiny Tapeout tile holds about a thousand standard cells. The designs on this site use a few dozen at most, so dozens of them fit in one tile. This chip gathers learners' designs side by side: every design gets the chip's inputs, and a selector on five spare pins picks which one drives the outputs. It is how Tiny Tapeout itself shares a chip, on a smaller scale.

Every design here passed the same checks as the studio, and the whole chip runs through real chip tools on every change.

The last mile: from files to a real chip

      
    
        
  1. Files. Your design, or the whole community chip, becomes a Tiny Tapeout project: Verilog, a pin list (info.yaml), a test and a datasheet. Download them below, or from the studio's last step.
    2. 
        
  2. A repository. Tiny Tapeout gives a template repository on GitHub; the files go into a copy of it. Its automatic checks run the real open-source flow (synthesis, placement and routing, design-rule checks) and show the finished layout.
    3. 
        
  3. A run. Tiny Tapeout collects designs into shuttle runs on open processes (SkyWater 130 nm, IHP 130 nm, GF 180 nm). Each run has a deadline; a tile is paid for when submitting. 
    4. 
        
  4. The wait. Roughly six to nine months from deadline to silicon is normal, because many designs share one schedule through the fab, packaging and testing.
    5. 
        
  5. What comes back. A small board with the shared chip on it. Select your design, set the switches, and test it on the bench, against its simulation, the way real chips are checked.
    6. 
      

      
Whether and when the community chip is submitted is decided by the project's maintainers once it holds enough designs; nothing is promised yet. Run dates change, so Tiny Tapeout's runs page is the place to check.
