Beyond von Neumann

The bottleneck is not the math

Almost every computer since 1945 follows John von Neumann's plan: memory in one place, a processor in another, and a busy road of data between them. Shrinking transistors made the math cheap, but the road did not get cheaper at the same pace. Today, fetching a number from memory can cost thousands of times more energy than doing arithmetic with it. Every new way to compute in this layer of the map attacks that road, or one of three other limits: the energy floor of a switch, the end of easy shrinking, and problems that ordinary logic handles badly.
