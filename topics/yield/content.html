In a real factory the rain is dust too small to see.

Rain is now defects, and chips are dies. Defects include dust, scratches and printing errors, and some land without doing harm. Cutting adds 10% silicon for the links between pieces. Leading 2 nm-class wafers are widely reported near $30,000; treat the price as an assumption.

Hollow dots are simulated wafers and the amber dot is the formula. For big dies in clumps, dots land a little under the curve: a big die can straddle two patches, and the formula treats it as sitting in one. Only known-good pieces are paired.

  • Left out: packaging and testing costs
  • Left out: spare parts that repair a die
  • Left out: defect rates falling as a factory learns
  • Left out: speed and power of the links
