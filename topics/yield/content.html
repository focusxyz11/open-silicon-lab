Wafer & yield

Why big chips are expensive and chiplets help

A 300 mm wafer holds many copies of the chip, called dies. Random defects land on the wafer. Any die with a defect is scrap. The bigger the die, the more likely it catches one, so the share of good dies (yield) falls fast as area grows. There is also a hard ceiling: a scanner prints at most 26 × 33 mm in one shot, the reticle limit.

Change the die size and defect density, then split the design into chiplets to see the cost of a working chip drop.
