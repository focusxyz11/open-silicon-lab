Computing with light

Steer light, and it does the math

Light can carry many signals at once, travels without heating wires, and adds up waves by itself. A Mach-Zehnder interferometer splits light into two paths, delays one path by a tunable phase, and recombines them. Depending on the phases, the light leaves by one output, the other, or both: the device multiplies the input by a small matrix. Meshes of these build bigger matrices, the core of AI math. The hard parts are getting numbers into light and back out, and keeping phases precise.

An ideal, lossless interferometer with two 50 : 50 couplers. Total power out always equals power in: it can only redirect light, so a mesh computes unitary matrices and needs extra tricks for general ones. Phase error is random, as from heat or fabrication. Real photonic processors also lose power in waveguides and spend energy in lasers, modulators and converters.
