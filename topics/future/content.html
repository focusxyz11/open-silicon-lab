Five forces shaping the next decade

This is a synthesis of the roadmaps above, not a forecast from any one company.

Energy per operation is the real limit

AI racks are now limited by power and cooling more than by transistor count. Backside power, lower-voltage transistors, FP4 math and optical links all attack joules per operation.

Density moves from shrinking to stacking

Gate pitch stops shrinking much after A10 on imec's roadmap. Gains come from stacking: CFET puts transistors on transistors, hybrid bonding stacks dies, and HBM stacks memory.

Memory bandwidth outranks raw compute

Compute has grown faster than memory bandwidth for years. Expect more HBM per chip, custom HBM base dies, and specialized chips for different phases of AI work.

Wires turn into light

Co-packaged optics put optical engines next to the chip so racks and data centers can link thousands of GPUs without copper's power cost. 2026 is the year it reaches commercial products.

Chips start designing chips

In 2026 all three big EDA vendors launched AI agents that run long design and verification loops on their own. Engineers increasingly set goals and review results while agents iterate.

Many futures, not one roadmap

A roadmap draws one line, but the future of chips depends on bets that could each go well or badly: whether new lithography arrives on time, whether stacked transistors work, whether there is enough memory and electricity, and how much AI speeds up chip design itself. Flip the switches and watch how AI chips' efficiency, their cost, and the world's total AI computing power could diverge by 2040.

Beyond silicon: what is real, and when?

New ideas for computing appear in the news every week. Most are years from products, and some will never get there.
