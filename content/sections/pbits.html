Probabilistic computing

Make the noise do the work

As transistors shrink, they get noisier, and chip designers spend energy fighting that noise. Probabilistic computing does the opposite. A p-bit flips randomly between 0 and 1, but its odds are steered by its neighbors. Connect p-bits with the right weights and the whole network spends most of its time in states that satisfy the rules you wired in. Clamp some bits, and the others settle into answers. Because the network has no fixed direction, the same circuit can run forwards and backwards.

How often each state A B C appeared

Three p-bits A, B and C with fixed couplings chosen so that the states where C = A AND B have the lowest energy. Each update re-draws one free bit at random, biased by its neighbors. With weak steering the bits wander almost randomly; with strong steering they settle, but can get stuck. Chips built this way target sampling, optimization and some generative AI models.
