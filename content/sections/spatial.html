Dataflow & wafer-scale

Bring the work to the data

A classic processor fetches instructions and data one step at a time. A spatial, or dataflow, chip lays the program out across hundreds of thousands of small tiles, each with its own memory. Weights stay put; only the activations flow from tile to tile. Wafer-scale chips push this to the limit with a whole wafer as one chip; SRAM-based inference chips do it across many smaller chips. The limit is capacity: when the model no longer fits on chip, the expensive road to DRAM comes back.

Each tile holds 2 MB of SRAM. Energy counts only reading the 8-bit weights once per pass, at 45 nm per-bit costs (DRAM about 40 pJ per bit, SRAM under 1 pJ). It ignores activations, the network between tiles and batching, which lets von Neumann machines reuse each fetched weight many times.
