Neuromorphic chips

Compute only when something happens

A brain runs on about 20 watts because most neurons are quiet most of the time. Neuromorphic chips copy that: each neuron collects incoming spikes, slowly leaks charge, and fires a spike of its own only when it crosses a threshold. No spikes, almost no energy. That makes them strong for always-on sensing, where nothing happens most of the time, and weak for dense number-crunching like large language models.

Two leaky integrate-and-fire neurons, each connected to four input sensors with fixed weights: detector A listens to sensors 1 and 2, detector B to 3 and 4, and each is inhibited by the other pair. A spiking chip only works when an input spike arrives; a conventional chip multiplies every weight at every time step. Real neuromorphic chips have millions of neurons and learn their weights.
