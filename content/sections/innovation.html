What AI speeds up, what needs invention, what waits on people

Every step in the chain has a different limiting factor. Some steps are mostly data and computation, so AI is already compressing them. Some are limited by physics and materials and need real inventions. Some are limited by money and construction time. Some are limited by people: skills, judgment, accountability and rules. A chain moves only as fast as its slowest link, so the kind of limit on each step tells you where the next gains will come from.

What this means

AI accelerates

Design gets cheaper and faster

Coding, verification, layout, lithography software and inspection are data problems. Expect smaller teams to design competitive chips, and more custom chips for specific AI models. The scarce resource shifts to proprietary data and to people who can judge the results.

Needs invention

Physics sets the pace of progress

Brighter EUV sources, better resists, stacked CFET transistors, 2D materials, optical links and denser memory stacks need new science. AI speeds up simulation and materials search, but each idea still has to be proven in a lab and then a fab, over years.

Needs capital & time

Capacity is the real bottleneck

Fabs, packaging lines, HBM plants and data-center power all take billions of dollars and two or more years to add. Demand for AI has repeatedly outrun them; CoWoS packaging and HBM were the tightest bottlenecks of 2024–2026.

Needs people & rules

Humans stay on the critical path

Someone must sign off a multimillion-dollar tape-out, decide what to build, and run fabs that face a shortfall of up to 157,000 US workers by 2030. Export controls, subsidies and standards decide who can build and buy what.
