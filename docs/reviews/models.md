# Review: the models behind the interactive numbers

Reviewed 2026-11-14 against `src/engines/chip.js`, `src/engines/fab.js`, `src/engines/life.js`, `src/engines/supply.js`, `src/data/world.js`, `src/engines/tinychip.js`, `src/engines/design.js`, `src/v2/game.js`, `src/v2/play.js`, `src/i18n/en.json`, `src/data/facts.json` and `tests/*.test.mjs`. Every model was run across all its settings (read only) and the numbers below are what it returned. Line numbers are those of the files on this date.

Outside checks made (five pages read; everything else is general device and industry knowledge and is marked "from memory" where a figure rests on it):

- Tom's Hardware, reporting TSMC's own figures for its N2 process against N3E: 10 to 15% more speed at the same power, 25 to 30% less power at the same speed, 1.15× the density for a mixed design and up to 1.2× for logic alone.
- CSET (Georgetown), "AI Chips: What They Are and Why They Matter" (2020), as quoted on CSET's own page about it: estimated foundry price per 300 mm wafer of $3,984 at 16/12 nm, $5,992 at 10 nm, $9,346 at 7 nm and $16,988 at 5 nm.
- Google Research, "Cores that don't count" (Hochschild and others, HotOS 2021), abstract: silent wrong answers in a large fleet come from defects in processors that manufacturing tests missed; such cores are "extremely rare".
- SemiWiki, "DDR vs. LPDDR vs. HBM": about 1.2 TB/s for one HBM3E stack, about 51 to 77 GB/s for one DDR5 module. It gives no energy per bit.
- EE News Europe on the CSET report (confirms the 5 nm figure and "a near doubling" from 7 nm; nothing more).

One page could not be opened: the CERN RADNEXT article "From MOSFETs to FinFETs: the soft error scaling trends" (radnext.eu), wanted for the memory upset rate. That figure stays unconfirmed.

## Summary

- **Chip model (`chip.js`): right directions, sizes from about 2005.** Every trend points the right way, but the shrink rules are the classical ones: 2× the switches, 25% faster and constant switching power per area each generation, with leakage as the only price. Today a generation gives about 1.2 to 1.6× the density and 10 to 15% more speed, wafers cost about 1.5× more, and leakage per switch is held level. Nine items are misleading, most of them in size, in the reason given or in the words.
- **Life model (`life.js`): sound textbook physics, one false lesson.** Ageing, heat, supply dip, escapes, weak chips and wire wear all behave believably. But the data centre can only be passed by duplicating all the logic, which no data-centre chip does, and its story contains a sum that is wrong by a factor of about 400.
- **Supply model (`supply.js`, `world.js`): the rule is fair, three entries in the data teach the wrong ranking.** Stopping both Taiwan sites only strains an AI accelerator's trip, while stopping the scanner maker stops it at once.
- **Tiny-chip engine (`tinychip.js`, `design.js`): sound.** Cell sizes, transistor counts, delays and mask count are right for a 130 nm library and the steps are described truthfully. Only the speed it reports (700 to 1,500 MHz) is misleading, and badly so for the core.

Most in need of fixing, in order:

1. Data centre needs lockstep logic to pass, and "one in a billion per hour happens every day" is false for the fleet shown (life L1, L2).
2. Closing Hsinchu and Tainan shows "strained" for an AI accelerator; closing Veldhoven shows "stopped" (supply S1, S2).
3. The shrink rules: density, speed and wafer cost per generation, and leakage shown per switch when the formula is per area (chip C1, C2, C4, C5, C8).
4. One model, two answers: "50 days" means 50 layers while the same screen shows 55 days; "{moving}% of the energy" beside a power that has no memory in it; a clock multiplier printed as GHz (chip C9, C12, C13).
5. "It could run at about 1,520 MHz", and the core's speed taken from one slice of its adder (tiny chip T3, T4).

Count: 48 items; sound 12, simplified but fair 19, misleading 16, not judged 1.

## 2. A chip's life

`src/engines/life.js`; goals in `MISSIONS` and in `src/v2/game.js` (`CHALLENGES` of kind `life`, `checkLife` at 162). A design signed off for a typical chip at 25 °C is sent to a phone, a car or a data centre. Five choices (margin, power grid, hot test, burn-in, protection) change four causes of failure over the years, the silent wrong answers from radiation, the speed and the cost.

What the model returns: the untouched design fails everywhere (half the chips fail the test, all the rest fail in the field). The phone passes with 15% margin, the grid and a hot test (0.03% failed, cost 1.2×). The car passes with exactly one combination: 30% margin, grid, hot test, burn-in and lockstep logic (66 per million failed, cost 1.97×). The data centre passes only with lockstep logic and at least 25% margin.

## 4. The tiny-chip engine and the design flow

`src/engines/tinychip.js` and `src/engines/design.js`. A description of a few lines is parsed, checked against its job, mapped onto eleven cells of a 130 nm library, placed in rows by simulated annealing, wired by a maze router on two layers, and its test patterns are graded against stuck wires. The numbers a learner sees: gates, transistors, speed in MHz, steps of wire, vias, masks, patterns and the share of faults caught.

What the engine returns for the lab's own solutions: half adder 2 cells, 18 transistors, 1,520 MHz; full adder 5, 42, 1,020 MHz; display decoder 24, 120, 943 MHz; ALU slice 22, 148, 714 MHz; counter 11, 132, 719 MHz; 26 masks for all; coverage 100% for every solution (80 to 100% for the starters). ## Open risks

- **The fixes interact.** C1, C2, C4 and C8 change what every chip challenge can reach, and `tests/chip.test.mjs` checks that each can be met and by no single setting. I gave values that keep the starting chip at 1 and the trends honest, and checked `fast` and `battery` by hand; `cores`, `fed`, `quick` and `months` need re-tuning with the model running. I did not run `npm test`.
- **The cost of a switch is argued over.** "Level since 28 nm" is the common reading of public estimates; makers dispute it, and the CSET figures are from 2020. `WAFER_COST` in C8 is chosen to make a core about level in cost, not measured.
- **Density figures before N2 are from memory** (1.8× and 1.6× for logic, less for whole chips), as are the 28 nm wafer price, the defect density, the pin speed on shared chips, the library's cell widths and the server power split. Each is marked where used.
- **Memory energy and upset rates are unconfirmed.** The 0.25× for memory in the package and the 500 per megabit could each be off by a few times. Neither changes a verdict.
- **The generations have no names.** I placed them by their layer counts (about 40, 28, 16, 7 and 5 nm). If they are meant as something else, "today's rate" in C1, C2 and C8 applies to different steps, though not in a way that rescues 2× and 25% for the newest two.
- **Where words are filled.** I read `chip.show.ship` as printing layers as days from its text; I did not find the call that fills it in `src/v2/` or `src/app/` by search, so check which value is passed. I did not open the pages in a browser.
- **S2 changes the engine.** `capital` is a new idea in `status()`; `tests/weave.test.mjs` covers the present rule and will need a case for it. A lighter fix is data only (make both needs not `sole`), at the cost of no longer showing that these are the only makers.
- **L1 has two possible fixes.** Raising the limit to 2,000 is the smallest. The fuller one is a third kind of protection for the data centre (software that re-checks results), which is what is really done, but it is a new choice and new words.
- **The earlier site.** `fab.js` also carries print choices and an AI option (`PRINT`, `VAR_AI`) that the chip world does not reach; not reviewed. `tinychip.js`'s other processes likewise (T10).
- **Two models, two yields.** `life.js` assumes 10% of chips defective; `chip.js` gives 21% at the starting size. A learner who follows one chip through both could notice. Passing the chip model's yield into `live()` would join them; I left it as a simplification.
- **What I let stand.** A phone chip at "full speed" all the time; a fleet that never changes; a fab with eight tool groups; a trip with seven or eight steps. All are fair for intuition and none teaches a wrong trend.
