# Review: the gate (logic gate) level

Reviewed 2026-11-13 against `src/i18n/en.json`, `src/data/facts.json`, `src/data/weave.js`, the gate scene in `src/app/scenes.js`, the timing model in `src/engines/chip.js` and the cell library in `src/engines/tinychip.js`. No web search was used; judgements rest on general circuit knowledge and the entries already in `facts.json`. Nothing has been applied: this file is the only change.

Reviewer: Claude Fable 5.2, asked with a written brief.

## Status

Applied 2026-11-13: all 20 suggested replacements, and the gate drawing (src/app/scenes.js) rewired as a working two-input NAND: on the upper strip the middle contact takes power and both ends feed the output. Still open: the figure in stop.idea.gate.note needs a source; texts elsewhere that call every cell a "gate".

Reviewer: Claude Fable 5.2, asked by Claude Opus 5.5 with a written brief; applied by Claude Opus 5.5.

## Summary

The level is sound in outline: four switches, two strips of silicon, two gate strips and metal is a fair picture of a two-input gate in today's chips (CMOS), and nothing in it is flatly false. Of the 31 keys in scope, 11 are fine, 20 need a wording fix and 0 are outright wrong. Six of the 20 are misleading and should go first: the part labelled "gates" inside a level called "Gate" is the other meaning of the word, and nothing says so; the silicon strips are labelled "channels", though most of each strip is source and drain; the metal is said to carry the inputs, when two of the three wires drawn are power lines; "problems double in size" should be the work that doubles; quantum computers are given as the only thing tried for such problems; and "anything with two states" leaves out the condition that most candidates fail. The commonest plain-language gap is that "logic gate" is never said to be anything, and "cells" and "transistors" appear bare. `stop.think.gate.note`, as changed after the switch review, is right and agrees with the switch level; it needs only a small tightening.

Severity count: wrong 0, misleading 6, unclear 6, nit 8.

## Numbers needing a facts.json entry

No gate-level stop contains a digit, so `npm test` asks for no `FIGURES` entry here, and none of the suggested stop texts adds a digit. The quantities below are stated in words or filled in by engines.

- **Checking takes more effort than creating** (`stop.idea.gate.note`, "more effort checking a design than creating it"). A comparison, so a figure. The fact: industry surveys of chip projects have for years found that checking (verification) takes about half or more of project time. Credible source: the Wilson Research Group functional verification study, published every two years by Siemens EDA. Needs a source; then add it to `FIGURES['idea.gate']`.
- **Tick and slowest signal** (`chip.show.gate`, `{tick}` and `{path}`: in `src/engines/chip.js` the slowest path is 0.8 of a starting tick and scales with switch size to the power 0.8). Model constants, no entry and no obvious published figure. Needs a source, or a note that it is the model's rule, as the switch review asked for the leak constants.
- **Switches per cell** (`here.gate`, suggested "two to a couple of dozen"; `depth.gate.see`, "four switches"). These come from the lab's own library in `src/engines/tinychip.js` (inverter 2, NAND 4, XOR 12, flip-flop 24) and match real 130 nanometre libraries in order of size. No entry is required while the text says "your design"; if it is ever stated as an industry fact, the open library's own documentation is the source. Needs a source if used that way.
- **Doubling** (`stop.limit.gate.q`, `stop.limit.gate.note`). Arithmetic, not an industry figure: no entry needed. The open question itself (whether such problems must be hard) has no entry either; `turing-1936` backs a different limit, at the rack. An entry is optional. Needs a source if added.

## Open risks

- **The picture does not wire a working gate.** In `src/app/scenes.js` (the `gate` scene) the lower strip is right for a NAND: two switches in a row between the low power line and the output. On the upper strip the left and the middle contacts both go to the high power line and only the right one to the output, so the left switch has both ends on the same line and does nothing; with the first input low and the second high, nothing drives the output. In a real NAND the middle contact goes to the power line and both ends to the output (or the reverse). This is code, outside this review, but an engineer will see it, and it is why no suggested text names the gate as a NAND.
- **No input wires are drawn.** The suggested `what.gate.metal` describes what is on screen: two power lines and the output. If input wires are added to the picture, the text should say so again.
- **One picture for three worlds, again.** Flat strips crossed by gate strips are right for the 130 nanometre library the learner's design uses and for most car chips. In the newest chips the strips are fins or stacks of sheets, as the switch level shows one step down. The words suggested here stay true for both, but the two pictures do not match each other.
- **Which parts light for which stop.** `stop.limit.gate` lights `diff`, which has nothing to do with its question. `stop.fail.gate` lights `gates`; "No two of these gates come out identical" is true of logic gates and of the gate strips alike, so I left it, but with the new label "switch gates" the reader may take it the second way. `NOTE_AT.gate` pins the timing note on `gates` as well.
- **"Gates" that are cells, outside scope.** `chipx.yours.print`, `chipx.yours.wires`, `design.cap.gates.d`, `game.built` and `game.tape.gates` count cells and call them gates. For a design with a flip-flop (the counter, the register bit) that is loose: a flip-flop is not a gate. The suggested `here.gate` defines a cell as "a ready-made logic gate or other small circuit"; `depth.gates.see` and `what.gates.cells` already say something close, and the wording should be one.
- **Names now disagree elsewhere if the suggestions are applied.** `chip.gen.notice`, `chip.show.block` and `here.switch` say "switch"; `chipx.yours.etch` says "transistors" bare, as `chipx.yours.gate` did. "Silicon strips" is new; the factory stations may name the same thing differently. The switch level's "channel", "source" and "drain" then fit under it without change.
- **"Can't be patched" elsewhere.** If `stop.idea.gate.q` changes to "the silicon ... can't be changed", the case attached to this stop (`fdiv`, 1994, a recall) still fits. Other texts that say a chip cannot be fixed were not searched for.
- **The level's name.** "Gate" is short and matches Block, Switch and Atom, so I left it. "Logic gate" as the name would remove the clash at its root; that is a design decision.
- **Simplifications I let stand.** "Each is slightly faster or slower than its neighbour" (true, and the spread grows as switches shrink). "Rewire the same switches and you get another" (true of the two-input NAND and NOR, which use the same four switches). "Teams often spend more effort checking a design than creating it" (the usual reading of the surveys; see the numbers section). "Every signal must settle before the next tick" (leaves out the small extra time a memory cell needs before the tick, which the tiny-chip engine does model).
- **The timing model.** I judged `chip.show.gate` on its words. That smaller switches are always faster, with wires ignored, is a simplification of the model; in recent generations wires take a growing share of the delay.
- **Tests not run.** I did not run `npm test`. The figures test looks only for digits in stop texts; the suggestions add none. If any test pins part labels or text lengths, the two new labels ("switch gates", "silicon strips") and the longer `depth.gate.see` should be checked at phone width.
- **No outside check.** Statements about verification effort, fluid and DNA logic and quantum computing are from general knowledge, not looked up. Each should be confirmed when its `facts.json` entry is written.
