# A complete review of the whole site, 25 November 2026

This review of the code, content and design was asked for by #354. It reviewed `main` at `dd024ff`.
The review was done by Claude Code. Ten review agents each read and ran one part of the site, and every
finding below was then checked and filed by hand. Each problem was reproduced in node or in the browser,
seen on a screenshot, or is certain from the lines named. The report only reports: the fixes come through the issues
it filed. There was no earlier complete review to compare with.

One pull request merged while the review ran, #360 (the first page on wide screens). It fixed one finding
(VM-1), which is marked below and was left out of the issues.

## Health

- **`npm test`**: 510 of 510 pass.
- **CI on `main`**: green. Run 699 on `dd024ff`: every browser group, the logic and words, and the deploy.
- **The full browser smoke test, run locally**: green where CI is green; the failures were the machine.
  - It ran on 4 CPUs while the review's own browser agents ran beside it, at load averages of 25 to 43.
    explore-desktop, explore-phone and the joins timed out, and the run stopped.
  - Rerun on a quiet machine, 10 of the 12 sections that had not finished passed. explore-desktop ("the
    first challenge did not open with its gates to tap") and motion ("the views did not zoom in and out")
    each failed one timing check. The labs section passed in a separate run (574 s).
  - On this machine the software renderer alone took about 350% CPU, and sections ran 3 to 5 times slower
    than on CI (explore-desktop 425 s here, 87 s there). The same commit is green on CI, so these are not
    counted as findings. If they show on CI as "passed only on a second run", look at those two checks first.
- **What was clean everywhere.** In every crawl, on every page and in every language, at 1280 dark and
  390 light:
  - no page errors, console errors or raw word keys, and no "Missing text";
  - no sideways scroll on the phone;
  - every view has a way back, and every lab draws and responds to its main control.
- **The principles that held.** The thirteen core questions are never shown together; Hold meets eleven of
  them, one per section. Every `part.*` has its `what.*` (157 of 157). `hold.css` stays scoped to its page.
  Hold (426 keys) and the blueprint (198) are complete in zh, fr and es, with the same placeholders, tags and
  figures as the English.
- **What holds up best.** The dive from campus to atom is continuous and consistent. The line steps cleanly
  through its eleven stations. A lab's Do mode puts the instrument on the stage with its goal as gauges, as
  `docs/SYSTEM.md` asks. Hold's type, its square of silicon and its chart are of a higher order than the
  rest, which is part of finding VM-J1.

**In short:** the site is healthy where the checks look, and most real problems are where they don't:
- **What a slow load or a quick move does:** a view opening after you left it (SH1), and words not loaded
  on Data Saver (DW1).
- **What the phone squeezes:** the cards under Hold's walls, the bench, four labs.
- **Whether the words are true:** the leak wall's physics stated backwards (CO1), two years for one bug
  (CO2).
- **Listeners and GPU memory that pile up over a visit.**

## What the review could not do

- **Six of Hold's sources could not be fetched**: dc-electricity, design-cost-2nm, design-cost-older,
  fab-module-cost, node-n2 and asic-vs-cpu. Their hosts are outside this session's network. Nine others
  were fetched and confirmed: energy-moving-data, transistor-trend (Hold's data matches Karl Rupp's raw
  data), rubin-gpu, clock-stall, wafer-space-run, high-na-price, moore-1965, fdiv-rate and fdiv-charge.
  #378 asks for the rest to be checked.
- **Some code was only skimmed:** the older fab engines (litho, etch, fabsteps, the implant and overlay
  physics), clockpower, aibuild, dope, `lens3d.js`, and journey3d's per-station builders. The words in
  `labs.json`, `place.json` and `atlas.json`, beyond the figures, were only sampled.
- **The full island-to-atom walk on the phone was not done**, nor Do and Read on the phone for level views
  (at rest the phone shows only a peek of the card). Pop-ins and stuck animations can't be judged reliably on
  the software renderer, so only clear cases are reported.
- **Several issues hold more than one finding.** The review was asked for at most 20 `task` issues, so small
  related findings share an issue, and none was left out for lack of room.
